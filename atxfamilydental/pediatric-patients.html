When should kids start?

The first visit can be simpler than you think.

We love seeing young patients and making sure they get the best dental care while having fun. ATX Family Dental is trained to see everyone in the family regardless of age — whether it’s a child’s first checkup or a routine visit.

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) recommends children have their first dental visit by age one or within six months of the first tooth appearing. An early visit helps establish a foundation for lifelong oral health.

A first visit is about more than teeth. For young children, becoming familiar with the dental environment and building trust are important parts of the experience.
What to expect

Early appointments are about comfort, familiarity, and prevention.

At a young age, dental visits are more about building familiarity and comfort with the dental environment than actual treatment.

01 · FIRST VISIT

Around age 1

Your child’s first visit might be brief. The dentist will check for signs of cavities or issues like teething problems, examine the gums, and may offer advice on infant oral care, including cleaning gums and introducing brushing with a soft toothbrush.

02 · ROUTINE CHECK-UPS

Ages 2–3

As your child’s teeth come in, the dentist can conduct a more thorough check-up, clean the teeth, look for cavities, and check for early signs of orthodontic concerns such as misalignment.

Why early visits matter

Healthy habits start with positive experiences.

Early dental care can support both physical oral health and a child’s comfort with the dentist. The goal is to make future visits feel familiar rather than intimidating.

Prevents cavities & oral health issuesEarly visits can help identify potential problems before they become more serious.
Guidance on oral hygieneParents can develop good daily habits that reduce the risk of cavities and gum disease.
Early awareness of orthodontic concernsDental visits can identify concerns such as crooked teeth or overbites that may need monitoring.
Comfort with the dental officeStarting early helps children become accustomed to the people, sounds, and environment of dental care.
Positive associationsFamiliarity can help children develop a more positive attitude toward oral health and future visits.
Emotional securityGentle, non-threatening experiences can help build trust and a sense of security.
